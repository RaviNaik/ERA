01 / 08 · THE WARNING

A wrong shift makes a beautiful curve

A target shifted the wrong way — or not at all — hands the model the answer. The loss falls smoothly. The model learns to copy its input. Nothing raises an exception. The only real defense is to decode the tokens on both sides and read them.

✅ The shift, verified — inputs beside targets 0 / 12 mismatches

Every target[i] is the token at input[i+1]. Sub-word pieces ('Sen' → 'j' → 'ō' = “Senjō”) line up one step apart.

❌ The bug this rules out — unshifted targets target = input

With no shift, position 0 is asked to predict 'Sen' from 'Sen'. Loss plummets. The model has learned nothing but the identity map.

02 / 08 · THE MECHANISM

The loss spine, end to end

One hidden state per position becomes one score per vocabulary token, then a probability distribution, then a single question: what probability did you give the token that actually came next?

💡 Perplexity
perplexity = exp(mean loss) — “how many equally-likely options is the model effectively choosing between.” An untrained model should sit near the vocabulary size. Ours: loss 10.93 → perplexity 55,984 against V = 50,257. A 1.0% gap. If it were far off, the target alignment is broken and nothing downstream can be trusted.
03 / 08 · SETUP

A 50M-parameter transformer on WikiText-103

Decoder-only, written from scratch — RMSNorm, pre-norm residual stream, SwiGLU FFN, causal multi-head attention. The trunk returns hidden states only; the output head is a separate module, which is what makes tied-vs-untied and the Part-2 second head drop-in changes.

04 / 08 · PART 1

The seven numbers

Each check prints the evidence for its own claim rather than asserting it. Click a card to expand.

🧮 Check 7 in detail — ordinary vs. chunked cross-entropy same loss to 9.5e-7

Ordinary materialises the full [16384, 50257] logits tensor and its gradient at once. Chunked processes 1,024 tokens at a time and back-propagates each chunk before computing the next, so autograd frees each slice's graph. The arithmetic is identical; only what must be resident changes.

05 / 08 · PART 2

A second head that predicts t+2

Two untied, architecturally identical heads on the shared trunk. The only difference is the target. Keeping them identical isolates the one effect the assignment asks about: how much harder is it to predict two tokens ahead?

Head losses over 4,000 steps L2 > L1 at 100% of steps
📊 Reading the gap
L2 − L1 starts at +0.06 (both heads random) and opens to +1.27 nats by step 3,999 — mean +1.13 after warm-up, never negative. In perplexity: the model chooses among ~112 options for the next token but ~400 for the one after. Both curves still fall together — they share one trunk.
