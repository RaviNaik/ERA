How every activity serves one goal

The goal: train a language model from scratch that is strong at Indian languages, coding, maths and science, and that behaves as an agentic assistant. Twelve teams each own one piece. This page explains, piece by piece and week by week, what is done and why it matters. It is built from the Lab 2 briefing, the course sessions (S17 on SFT in particular), the group chats and our own work. Dates are IST, 2026.

1. The goal and why it is hard

A model is only as good as five things: the text it reads, the way that text is cut into tokens, the network that learns from it, the hardware time spent well, and the training after pre-training that turns it into an assistant. A weakness in any one shows up in the final model. Three examples that drive many decisions here:

  • Indic scripts break easily. Zero-width joiner and non-joiner characters (ZWJ/ZWNJ) change how a Hindi, Bengali or Malayalam word is shaped. A cleaning step that drops them, or a Unicode normalisation (NFKC) that rewrites them, silently damages the language. So every cleaning rule is tested against a gold set before it is allowed near the data.
  • A tokenizer decides how much Indic text costs. If a language needs many tokens per word (high fertility), the model sees less of it per training step and pays more to serve it. The current tokenizer baseline has mean fertility 2.8355 on FLORES, and the first new candidate is 15.96% better on Indic text while English is only 1.98% worse (the limit is 2%).
  • Scores must be honest. If benchmark questions leak into training data, the score means nothing. Decontamination and licences are treated as hard gates, not paperwork.

2. The pipeline end to end

Raw sources→Clean + dedup (G1, G2)→Tokenizer (G3) + KE2 codes (G4)→Model config (G5) + speed (G6)→Pre-training on shared compute (G7, G8)→SFT + RL (G10)→Quantize + serve (G12)→Release (G11)

Evaluation (G9) sits across the whole chain: it supplies the benchmarks that tell every other team whether a change helped, and the fingerprints that keep benchmark text out of training data.

Where our team fits. Pre-training gives a base model that only predicts the next token. SFT is the step that turns it into an assistant: it reasons when needed, calls tools, answers once and stops. The loss is the same next-token loss, but the data is conversations and agent trajectories in one fixed chat format, and only the tokens the assistant writes are trained on. After SFT, RL scores attempts with checkers (tests, references) and pushes the model towards the ones that pass. Everything G10 produces is the raw material for those two stages.

8. G10 Post-training and Agentic, in depth

The Session 17 lesson sets the question we answer: what exactly do we train on, in what format and with what settings, so the model becomes a strong coding and agentic assistant that also works in Indian languages?

A. Chat format and renderer (task A)

What: ChatML with four roles (system, user, assistant, tool). Every structural marker is one special token: <|im_start|>, <|im_end|>, think and tool-call markers. Effort is a plain text line at the top of the system turn, Reasoning effort: 4k, levels 1k, 4k, 16k, 64k. render.py turns a conversation into token IDs and a loss mask, and rejects a literal <think> typed as text.

Why it matters: training and serving must use the identical format, or quality falls. Special tokens make turn boundaries safe: a user cannot fake a system turn by typing text. The loss mask is what teaches the model to write only assistant text. The slot map is shared with G3: the g10 slots start at ID 130,822. Slots 05 and 06 (subagent call and return) still need renaming by G3.

Deadlines: chat template to G12 Thu 12 Nov; render tests on the frozen tokenizer Sat 14 Nov.

B. Licence table (the data gate)

What: every candidate SFT or RL set gets a row: licence, who or what generated the text, and a decision. In: permissive and CC-BY-SA with attribution, open-weight generators with permissive output terms (for example gpt-oss). Out: non-commercial, no-derivatives, unstated licences, text from closed OpenAI, Anthropic or Google models or from Llama models, untraceable sources.

Why it matters: a teacher's output carries the teacher's terms. If we train on data we may not use, the final model cannot be released (G11) and the work is lost. Our fork PR #20 closed three gaps: three Nemotron sets with no row at all (up to 22% of the mix), NuminaMath-1.5 with no single generator (excluded), and the Aya collection narrowed to 12 templated Indic subsets with the rest excluded. Still missing: sections for two members.

C. Conversion to chat format (task B, convert.py)

What: adapters read each licensed open dataset, render it with render.py, mark loss spans (assistant bodies plus the end marker), derive an effort label from the think plus tool trace, and write a manifest per set (source, licence, generator, decision, rows, content hash). It refuses sets the licence table excludes. Four adapters exist (self-oss-instruct, codeforces-cots, swe-rebench-openhands, aya_dataset); the rest of the proposal's sets still need adapters.

Why it matters: this is the bridge from "datasets on the internet" to "tokens the trainer can use". The manifest makes every training row traceable, which is what the licence rule and decontamination both need.

D. Decontamination (task C)

What: decontam_check.py compares SFT/RL prompts against the G9 fingerprints (v2 due 4 Nov) and writes sft/decontam_report.md; rerun Fri 13 Nov.

Why it matters: Kolibri-1 removed only 0.005% of rows, but we still check every source against every benchmark we report, because a score is only useful when it can be trusted.

E. SFT mix proposal (lead and member B)

What: Code 35%, Agentic 25%, Tool use 15%, Indic 15%, General 10%. Effort tiers 1k/4k/16k/64k target 30/35/25/10. Four gates: licence and generator, decontamination, ChatML v0 with loss masks, script integrity (no NFKC, ZWJ/ZWNJ kept).

Why it matters: the shares are a starting guess, not a result. The Kolibri-1 report shows a larger math share did not help math and that 7.3% of generations in the math-heavy mix ended in loops. So the mix is settled later by proxy runs scored on IndicIFEval and English benchmarks together.

F. SWE environments and results (tasks D, E, F)

What: 20 software-engineering environments (a real repository, a task, tests) plus results/swe_gold_check.md, due Fri 13 Nov.

Why it matters: agentic coding is the most important SFT data for us. Trajectories that pass real tests become training data; the same environments later become RL tasks. OpenThoughts-Agent found the task source matters most (up to 30 points), that a few good sources beat many, and that dropping very short traces gave +3.5 points.

G. RL code tasks and verifier (task G)

What: 10 RL code tasks, rl/verifier.py and a test, due Fri 13 Nov.

Why it matters: RL needs a checker that scores an attempt without a human. A reliable verifier is what makes the RL stage possible and keeps it from rewarding wrong code.

H. Rotation members

Three rotation members each check five SFT/RL sets against the licence table by Sat 14 Nov. Their checks are an independent second look at our decisions.

10. Rules that protect quality

  • A medicine passes only if it changes or drops at most max(1 doc, 1%) of the gold set.
  • Harm tests count only on the real gold set; an empty gold is "not testable".
  • Never edit the gold to pass.
  • ZWJ and ZWNJ are never diseases.
  • Benchmark and evaluation text never goes into training data.
  • Work goes into the lab repo as a pull request that passes pr-guard; heavy jobs go through the TSAI job queue.
